1. Executive Summary

On August 26, 2026, a catastrophic Glacial Lake Outburst Flood (GLOF) and monsoon downpour surged through the high-gradient Trishuli River gorge in central Nepal. Leveraging authentic European Space Agency Sentinel-1 C-SAR repeat-pass observations orthorectified via ESA SNAP Range-Doppler Terrain Correction, VARUNA identified 126.60 km² of candidate standing water and 108.21 km² of coarsened debris/scour across the 1,526.05 km² Common Analysis Grid. Geodesic verification confirms negligible projection distortion (+0.015% WGS84 geodesic variance).

3. Critical Infrastructure Exposure
  • Building Structures: 1,062 of 3,816 surveyed structures (27.83%) intersect SAR hazard pixels (884 flood, 178 debris). Of 2,813 confirmed Destroyed structures in EMSR927, 936 structures (33.27%) intersect persistent SAR anomaly pixels.
  • Transportation Network: 35.12 km of 260.71 km surveyed road length (13.47%) intersect hazard zones (20.70 km water, 14.42 km debris, 0.00 km double-counted).
  • Pasang Lhamu Highway Corridor: 9.62 km of the 57.95 km lifeline corridor intersects hazard screening zones. Proximity audit confirms only 0.30 km lies within 50m of destroyed local riverbed access tracks.
OPERATIONAL DISCLAIMER & DATA PROVENANCE:
1. Potential Exposure vs Destruction: Satellite hazard intersections indicate potential exposure. They do not prove physical collapse or impassability without civil engineering ground inspection.
2. 10-Day Observation Lag: Post-event Sentinel-1 pass occurred September 5, 2026. High-gradient flood crests drain in 24–48h; SAR detects persistent standing water, widened beds, and saturated terraces.
3. Terrain Shadow Limitation: Auxiliary shadow bands were omitted during single-band dB export; backscatter increases on extreme cliffs (>25°) require cautious optical confirmation.
